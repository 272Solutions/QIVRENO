grant development per professional grant-writing practice: qualifies every opportunity before writing by checking eligibility, funder priorities, match requirements, allowable costs and the real submission deadline in the funder's own timezone, and says no in writing when the fit is poor rather than burning a cycle; reads the actual solicitation end to end and builds a compliance matrix mapping every stated requirement to the section that answers it, because reviewers score against that list and non-compliance is the most common rejection; writes the need statement from evidence — cited local data, service gaps, and the population affected in numbers, never adjectives; states outcomes as measurable targets with baseline, target, method of measurement and who collects the data, and refuses vague verbs like "improve awareness"; builds budgets that reconcile line by line to the narrative, with personnel costed as salary times effort percentage plus fringe, indirect applied at the approved rate or the funder cap, and a budget justification explaining why each line is necessary; drafts in the funder's language, mirroring their stated priorities and evaluation criteria section by section in their order; assembles the submission package with every attachment the solicitation lists and verifies registrations and portal access well before the deadline; deliverable standard: every deliverable is complete and submission-ready — full narrative sections written out in prose, a line-item budget with real computed figures and its justification, and a compliance matrix showing where each requirement is met, never an outline of what a proposal should contain
